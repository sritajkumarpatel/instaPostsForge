## Checklist

Mark each item passed or failed, using exactly these names:

1. accuracy: everything the caption describes is actually in the photo. Fail it only for a claim that is false or invented; a caption that leaves out one of the photos is not inaccurate. taken_at in the notes is verified from the photo's metadata, so a day, month, season or time that matches it is accurate.
2. hook: the first line is at most 90 characters and would make a viewer stop.
3. tone: the voice fits the mood playbook and uses none of its "avoid" words. For a deity, temple, puja or religious festival, it must be reverent: fail tone if a deity is described from outside or physically ("a goddess with many arms", "a statue"), if it jokes, or if it ends with a question instead of a devotional greeting.
4. length: always pass this item. Code counts the characters and holds the caption to $caption_max, so don't estimate or fault the length.
5. quote: there is no quote, or it fits the mood, is at most 140 characters, and any named author is certainly correct.
6. hashtags: 3 to 5 tags, all specific to this photo, none generic, no brand or private place.
7. alt_text: it plainly describes what is in the photo.
8. safety: nothing explicit, sexual, nude, hateful or otherwise against Instagram's guidelines, and none of the topics the standing instructions say this account never touches (politics, caste, religious debate, communal topics, war and conflict, controversy). A reverent devotional post is not religious debate. The account owner handpicked every photo: homes, balconies, rooms, cars and personal belongings are fine to show and to mention, so never fail safety for that.

## Hard fails

Fail the matching item whenever any of these is true:
- The caption names, describes or judges a person in the photo (safety).
- The caption or tags give a house number, street address or number plate (safety).
- A brand is named or tagged (safety).
- The caption, quote, tags or text on the image touch politics, caste, religious debate or comparison, communal topics, war or border tensions, or sexual innuendo (safety).
- An attributed quote whose author you are not certain of (quote).
- A hashtag in the caption text itself (hashtags).

## Text on the image

A styled post (a grid, poster, gallery frame or story) may have a few words drawn on the image, shown as "Text drawn on the image". It can't be changed after posting, so judge it as strictly as the caption: fail accuracy if it claims something the photos don't show, tone if it breaks the mood playbook or a devotional post's reverence, and safety for anything the hard fails below cover.

## Multi-photo posts

When you are shown several photos, they go out together, photo 1 first as the cover. Judge accuracy against every photo, and check that each photo has its own plain alt text. The playbook is the cover's.

Fail hook, tone, hashtags or alt_text only for a clear problem, not for a matter of taste; a caption you would have written differently still passes. Approve only if every item passes. If any fails, choose "revise" and write feedback that names each failed item and says exactly what to change, for example: "hook: 112 characters, cut to under 90". Don't rewrite the caption yourself.
